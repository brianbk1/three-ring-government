About Down the Middle

Down the Middle covers American government — the Executive branch, Congress, the federal courts, and state and local government — by showing how outlets on the left and the right actually covered the same story, then summarizing what verifiably happened, stripped of both sides' framing.

Most news-comparison tools stop at showing you two headlines side by side and calling it balance. That's a start, but it isn't the same as knowing what happened. Down the Middle goes a step further: for every story, a neutral summary is written separately from either side's framing, built from the underlying facts both sides generally agree on, with links to the original reporting so you can go read it yourself.

What's Under the Tent

  • Three Rings, one Sideshow — coverage is organized by branch: the Executive Ring (White House and federal agencies), the Legislative Ring (Congress), the Judicial Ring (the courts), and the Sideshow (states and local government).
  • The Spin Meter — a quick rating of how far apart left and right coverage sits on a given story, from mild spin to two completely different planets.
  • The Overturn-O-Meter™ — a live tracker of the Supreme Court's reversal rate for the current term.
  • Topic filters — narrow coverage to geopolitical, markets, entertainment, or legal angles on government news.
  • Coming Attractions — a forward-looking view of scheduled votes, hearings, rulings, and deadlines.

How Stories Are Found and Summarized

Every story is located through live web search of current reporting at the time you search — nothing is pre-written or cached from a fixed list. Each side's framing is described in our own words, not copied verbatim, with links back to the original articles for anyone who wants the full piece. The neutral summary is generated separately from either side's version, built to describe what happened rather than how either outlet wants you to feel about it.

What We Are — and Aren't

Down the Middle is a media literacy and news comparison tool, not a news wire and not a fact-checking authority. Framing descriptions and neutral summaries are AI-generated from real-time search results and can be imperfect. The point isn't to replace your own reading — it's to make the gap between left and right coverage visible enough that you can read the originals with better context.
