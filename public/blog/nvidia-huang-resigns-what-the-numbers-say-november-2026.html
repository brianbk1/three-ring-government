Jensen Huang Is Out at Nvidia. Here’s What the Numbers Say.

November 13, 2026 · Independent, nonpartisan analysis

On Thursday morning, Nvidia’s board announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately. Huang co-founded the company in 1993 and led it for 33 years. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, CFO since 2013, is now interim CEO while keeping the CFO job. Longtime director Harvey Jones became the board’s first-ever chair. [1]

The resignation came four days after the board opened an investigation into reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. That inquiry began with a New York Times report that U.S. officials believe a businesswoman close to him was a Chinese intelligence asset. Our Nov. 12 analysis covers how Huang gained his influence over U.S. chip policy. This post looks at what the data says now that he’s gone.

What happened

Two different kinds of claims drove this story, and they deserve different weight. Some things are documented: Huang acknowledged the affair in a note to staff, the board opened an investigation, and Huang resigned. [4] [1] Other things are still only reported: that the woman, a former executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed, was a Chinese intelligence asset, and that the relationship helped move chips to Chinese buyers. These claims rely on anonymous officials or reporting that hasn’t been verified. When the board opened its investigation, it said it had no reason to believe U.S. national security or Nvidia’s proprietary information had been compromised. [10]

Insight 1

Investors priced in the loss of Huang, not the loss of Nvidia’s business

The trading pattern on Thursday tells a story of its own. Shares fell as much as 14% right after the opening bell, then recovered most of that ground to close down 4.3%. [2] [3] Over the full week the stock still lost about a tenth of its value. But the rebound during the day suggests investors were separating two questions: how much was Huang personally worth to the company, and how much is the business itself worth. One analyst quoted by CBS put it this way: customers buy Nvidia’s chips, software and networking, and none of that leaves with the CEO. [3] The skeptics’ view is captured by Bernstein, which cut its price target in half. [2]

Insight 2

A governance gap made the exit sudden

Many founder-led tech companies are structured so the founder can’t be pushed out. Nvidia isn’t one of them. Huang owned about 3.6% of the company with no majority voting control. All 10 of the other directors are independent, and under the bylaws a simple majority could remove him. [5] That is why a scandal that started on Oct. 29 ended in a resignation just two weeks later.

Insight 3

U.S. chip policy loses its most powerful advocate, but the China market was already shrinking

Huang was the industry’s leading voice for selling chips to China. He pushed hard enough to win the 2025 reversal on the H20 chip and negotiated the unprecedented deal to pay the U.S. government 15% of China chip revenue. [15] [16] By this year, though, the business at stake had already shrunk. China’s share of Nvidia’s revenue fell from about 20% to single digits. In April, Huang said Nvidia’s share of China’s AI chip market had gone from roughly 90% to zero. [9] [17] So his departure removes a lobbyist from the policy fight more than it removes a revenue stream.

How each side is framing it

Same story, different emphasis

These are the focal points of each group’s on-the-record statements and actions, described in our own words.

Republican lawmakers

  • Their focus is national security: whether Huang was targeted by Chinese intelligence, and how well Nvidia guards against espionage. [6]
  • House Republicans requested a closed-door briefing on Nov. 18, and Mast threatened a subpoena. [6] [7]

Democrats and critics of the administration

  • Their focus is access: Huang’s close ties to the White House, the 15% revenue-share deal, Nvidia’s $25M ballroom pledge, and Trump’s disclosed trades in Nvidia stock. [18]
  • Members of both parties objected to the 15% deal back in 2025. [15]

The White House

  • Trump said Huang told him he had no idea the woman was a spy, and that he believed him. Trump has said the FBI is looking into it. [18]
  • The administration’s position is that selling older chips to China preserves U.S. leverage. [15]

Down the middle

Both sides’ concerns can be legitimate at once. The espionage question is serious, and still unproven. The governance question is documented and doesn’t depend on how the espionage question turns out: one executive had extraordinary influence over a national-security policy his company profited from. And Nvidia had no succession plan for a crisis. Huang’s exit settles none of these questions. It just means they will now be tested without him: in the FBI inquiry, in the board’s investigation, in Congress and in Nvidia’s earnings.

What to watch

Nov. 17: Nvidia’s third-quarter earnings, its first report since Huang left. Kress will deliver them as both interim CEO and CFO. [1] Nov. 18: the requested House briefing. It’s now unclear who would appear, since Huang no longer works for the company. [6] Ongoing: the board’s search for a permanent CEO, the FBI inquiry, the board’s own investigation, and the shareholder lawsuits. [1] [8] The test for readers is the same as before: watch what gets proven, not just what gets alleged.
